# Product Vision

## Working Title

Containment

The title is temporary and may change without affecting the product direction.

## Player and Context

The target player opens the game during a metro ride, a short break, or before sleeping.
They should be able to understand the core interaction quickly and complete two or three satisfying levels in a few minutes.

## Product Promise

Containment is a one-finger mobile arcade-puzzle game in which the player creates barriers, safely captures empty regions, and gradually restricts moving threats.

The game combines:

- the satisfaction of cleaning, filling, and completing an area;
- a small amount of timing tension while a barrier grows;
- short levels and immediate restarts;
- audiovisual feedback strong enough to make successful captures enjoyable by themselves.

## Emotional Target

The desired feeling is:

- calm concentration;
- light danger while a barrier is incomplete;
- relief and satisfaction when it locks;
- a visible before/after transformation when an area is captured;
- a strong desire to play one more short level.

The game should not feel like a harsh retro arcade game in the early content.

## Primary Reward Moment

The capture sequence is the equivalent of a merge moment in a merge game.

A strong capture should combine:

- readable barrier growth;
- rising but soft audio tension;
- a clear lock moment;
- a pleasant fill/clean animation;
- percentage progression;
- restrained camera/haptic feedback;
- an optional near-miss or large-capture celebration.

## Product Positioning

Do not present the product as a nostalgic JezzBall clone.
Treat the classic area-splitting mechanic as the underlying engine for a modern satisfying mobile experience.

## Not the Goal of the Vertical Slice

The first decision build is not intended to prove:

- monetization;
- live operations;
- account systems;
- online competition;
- large-scale content production;
- a final art style.

It is intended to answer:

> Is the barrier-and-capture interaction satisfying enough to justify producing the full game?
